# Worked examples: plan

One file per lecture, `examples/examplesN.tex` (N = 1..12, matching `lectureN.tex`, i.e.
Lectures 12–23). Each set has 4–6 fully worked problems whose purpose is *practice with
concrete calculation* of the lecture material: they are deliberately less exam-like than the
two problem sets, and they fill in calculations the lectures state without proof or leave
"as a matter of algebra". Numerical results are checked by scripts in `python/`
(`python/verify_examplesN.py`), and a few examples generate a figure.

Format (see `examples/template.tex`): title "Worked examples for Lecture N: title", a
two-sentence preamble, then `Example k` = boxed problem statement followed by the solution.
Style follows `STYLE.md`. Cross-references to the lecture notes use equation numbers of the
*revised* notes only where unavoidable; otherwise refer to named results ("the Christoffel
equation").

## examples1 — Lecture 12: Finite elasticity
1. **Simple shear.** Motion $\bphi(\mathbf{x},t)=(x_1+\gamma t\,x_2,\;x_2,\;x_3)$. Compute
   $\mathbf{v}$, $\mathbf{F}$, $J$; show $J=1$ (isochoric); spatial density equals referential
   density; compute the spatial velocity field $\mathbf{v}(\bphi^{-1}(\mathbf{y},t),t)$ and
   contrast it with the referential velocity; compute $\mathbf{C}=\mathbf{F}^T\mathbf{F}$ and
   its eigenvalues $\lambda_\pm=1+\tfrac12\gamma^2t^2\pm\gamma t\sqrt{1+\tfrac14\gamma^2t^2}$
   (principal stretches $\sqrt{\lambda_\pm}$, with $\lambda_+\lambda_-=1$).
2. **Polar decomposition by hand (2D).** $\mathbf{F}=\begin{pmatrix}2&1\\0&1\end{pmatrix}$
   (plane strain, third direction trivial). Compute $\mathbf{C}$, its eigenvalues
   $3\pm\sqrt5$ and eigenvectors, $\mathbf{U}=\sqrt{\mathbf{C}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{10}}\begin{pmatrix}6&2\\2&4\end{pmatrix}$ (an earlier draft of this plan had a wrong $\mathbf U$; the written example has the correct one, verified numerically: $\mathbf{U}=\mathbf{C}^{1/2}$ via the eigen-decomposition; $\mathbf{R}=\mathbf{F}\mathbf{U}^{-1}$ a rotation by angle $\theta$ with $\tan\theta=-1/3$; $\mathbf{V}=\mathbf{R}\mathbf{U}\mathbf{R}^T$). Draw the picture of Fig.~1 of Lecture 13 for this $\mathbf{F}$ with `python/ex1_polar_decomposition.py` (unit circle → ellipse; eigenvectors of $\mathbf{U}$ and of $\mathbf{V}$ shown; include as `examples/figures/ex1_polar.png`).
3. **Rigid motions.** $\bphi=\mathbf{a}(t)+\mathbf{Q}(t)\mathbf{x}$: show $\mathbf{F}=\mathbf{Q}$, $J=1$, $\mathbf{C}=\mathbf{I}$, kinetic energy $=\tfrac12 M\|\dot{\mathbf a}\|^2+$ rotational term, and that any $W$ satisfying material frame indifference has $W(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{Q})=W(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{I})$ so the potential energy is unchanged. Also show that $W(\mathbf{F})=\tfrac12\mu\,\mathrm{tr}(\mathbf{F}^T\mathbf{F}-\mathbf{I})$ is frame indifferent while $W(\mathbf{F})=\tfrac12\mu\,\mathrm{tr}(\mathbf{F}-\mathbf{I})$ and $W=\tfrac12\mu\|\mathbf{F}-\mathbf{I}\|^2$ are not (give an explicit $\mathbf{Q}$).
4. **Change of variables.** Body $M$ = unit cube, motion $\bphi=(\lambda_1x_1,\lambda_2x_2,\lambda_3x_3)$; compute mass, volume of $M_t$, spatial density; then the referential and spatial forms of the kinetic energy for $\lambda_i=\lambda_i(t)$ and check they agree.
5. **Hamilton's principle in one dimension.** Elastic rod $x\in[0,L]$, motion $\varphi(x,t)$, $F=\partial_x\varphi$, $W(F)=\tfrac12 E(F-1)^2$, Lagrangian density $\tfrac12\rho v^2-W$. Derive $\rho\,\partial_t^2\varphi=E\,\partial_x^2\varphi$ and natural boundary conditions $\partial_x\varphi=1$ at both ends; identify $T=E(F-1)$ as the first Piola–Kirchhoff stress and note the free-end condition $T=0$. Then linearise $\varphi=x+su$ to get the wave equation and speed $\sqrt{E/\rho}$.
6. **Traction as force per unit reference area.** For the uniform stretch of Example 4 with a strain energy $W=\tfrac12\kappa(J-1)^2$ (agent: compute $T_{ij}=\kappa(J-1)\,\partial J/\partial F_{ij}=\kappa(J-1)J F^{-T}_{ij}$), compute the traction on the face $x_1=1$ in the reference body and the corresponding Cauchy traction (force per unit *current* area), showing they differ by the factor $\lambda_2\lambda_3$.

## examples2 — Lecture 13: Constitutive theory
1. **St Venant–Kirchhoff material.** $U(\mathbf{C})=\tfrac12\lambda(\mathrm{tr}\,\mathbf{E})^2+\mu\,\mathrm{tr}(\mathbf{E}^2)$ with $\mathbf{E}=\tfrac12(\mathbf{C}-\mathbf{I})$. Compute $\mathbf{S}=2\partial U/\partial\mathbf{C}=\lambda(\mathrm{tr}\,\mathbf{E})\mathbf{I}+2\mu\mathbf{E}$, $\mathbf{T}=\mathbf{F}\mathbf{S}$; verify $\mathbf{F}^{-1}\mathbf{T}$ symmetric; linearise about $\mathbf{F}=\mathbf{I}$ to recover $A_{ijkl}=\lambda\delta_{ij}\delta_{kl}+\mu(\delta_{ik}\delta_{jl}+\delta_{il}\delta_{jk})$ (this is the "tedious algebra which we will skip" for one concrete isotropic $W$).
2. **Elastic fluid.** $W=V(J)$. Using $\partial J/\partial F_{ij}=J F^{-1}_{ji}$ (derive it from the cofactor expansion), show $\mathbf{T}=V'(J)J\mathbf{F}^{-T}$, identify the pressure $p=-V'(J)$ and show that the Cauchy stress is $-p\mathbf{I}$; linearise about a stress-free state ($V'(1)=0$) to obtain $A_{ijkl}=\kappa\delta_{ij}\delta_{kl}$ with $\kappa=V''(1)$.
3. **Material symmetry group.** Show directly from the definition $W(\mathbf{F}\mathbf{Q})=W(\mathbf{F})$ that the set of such $\mathbf{Q}$ is a group (closure, identity, inverses). Show that for $U(\mathbf{C})=f(\mathrm{tr}\,\mathbf{C},\mathrm{tr}\,\mathbf{C}^2,\det\mathbf{C})$ every rotation is a symmetry (isotropy), and that for $U=f(\mathbf{C}:\hat{\bm\nu}\hat{\bm\nu},\dots)$ only rotations about $\hat{\bm\nu}$ are (transverse isotropy).
4. **Counting components and Voigt notation.** Map $(ij)\to I\in\{1,\dots,6\}$; show $A_{ijkl}=A_{jikl}=A_{ijlk}=A_{klij}$ gives a symmetric $6\times6$ matrix (21 constants); write the isotropic tensor and the transversely isotropic tensor of the lecture (with $\hat{\bm\nu}=\hat{\mathbf{e}}_3$) as $6\times6$ matrices, hence identify the five independent TI constants $A_{1111}, A_{3333}, A_{1133}, A_{2323}, A_{1212}$ in terms of $\lambda,\mu,\gamma,\xi,\zeta$ (agent: compute and verify numerically with a script that builds the 81-component tensor).
5. **From the elastic tensor to the Navier equation.** Substitute the isotropic $A_{ijkl}$ with constant $\lambda,\mu$ into $\rho\ddot u_i=\partial_j(A_{ijkl}\partial_l u_k)$ and obtain $\rho\ddot{\mathbf u}=(\lambda+\mu)\nabla(\nabla\cdot\mathbf u)+\mu\nabla^2\mathbf u$; then show that in a *heterogeneous* isotropic body extra terms $\nabla\lambda(\nabla\cdot\mathbf u)+\nabla\mu\cdot(\nabla\mathbf u+\nabla\mathbf u^T)$ appear.
6. **Stress-free vs pre-stressed equilibrium (short).** For $U$ of Example 1 evaluate $\mathbf{T}$ for the uniform dilatation $\mathbf{F}=(1+\epsilon)\mathbf{I}$ to first and second order in $\epsilon$; show the first-order term reproduces $\kappa=\lambda+\tfrac23\mu$ (pressure $=-3\kappa\epsilon$ at first order).

## examples3 — Lecture 14: Seismic sources
1. **Stress glut of a fault in an isotropic body.** With $\overline S_{ij}=A_{ijk3}w_k\delta(x_3)$ and isotropic $A$, show $\overline S_{ij}=\mu(w_i\delta_{j3}+w_j\delta_{i3})\delta(x_3)$ (uses $w_3=0$); for slip $\mathbf w=D\hat{\mathbf e}_1$ uniform over a fault of area $\mathcal A$ concentrated at a point, define the moment tensor $M_{ij}=\int\overline S_{ij}\,\mathrm d^3\mathbf x$, obtain the double couple $M_{13}=M_{31}=\mu\mathcal A D=M_0$ and its eigenvalues $(M_0,0,-M_0)$ with eigenvectors at $45^\circ$ (P and T axes).
2. **Equivalent body force.** Compute $f_i=-\partial_j\overline S_{ij}$ for the point double couple $\overline S_{ij}=M_{ij}\delta(\mathbf x)$: $f_i=-M_{ij}\partial_j\delta(\mathbf x)$; show $\int f_i\,\mathrm d^3\mathbf x=0$ and $\int\epsilon_{ijk}x_jf_k\,\mathrm d^3\mathbf x=\epsilon_{ijk}M_{jk}=0$ (no net force or torque because $\overline S$ is symmetric); interpret as two force couples.
3. **Explosion.** $\overline S_{ij}=M_0\delta_{ij}\delta(\mathbf x)H(t)$: body force $-M_0\nabla\delta$; show that for an isotropic homogeneous body the displacement is irrotational (take the curl of the equation of motion, hence only P-waves are generated). *(Agent: keep to the level of taking the curl of the Navier equation with this force.)*
4. **Finite rupture and moment rate.** Fault of length $L$ along $x_1$, rupture propagating at speed $v_r$ with slip $w_1(x_1,t)=D\,H(t-x_1/v_r)$; compute the scalar moment rate $\dot M_0(t)=\mu W_{\rm f}\int\dot w_1\,\mathrm dx_1$ (with fault width $W_{\rm f}$): a boxcar of duration $L/v_r$; hence the total moment and a rough duration for $L=100\,$km, $v_r=3\,$km/s.
5. **Well- and ill-posedness in a linear problem.** Polynomial interpolation as a linear system $\mathbf V\mathbf c=\mathbf y$ (Vandermonde): (a) for $n=3$ points and $m=2$ show existence and uniqueness explicitly; (b) for $n=3$, $m=1$ derive the compatibility (collinearity) condition; (c) for $n=2$, $m=2$ exhibit the one-parameter family of solutions (null space); (d) numerically: the condition number of the $n\times n$ Vandermonde matrix for equally spaced points on $[0,1]$ for $n=5,10,15,20$ (`python`), to illustrate that "continuous dependence" can be practically useless.

## examples5 — Lecture 16: Ray theory
1. **The isotropic eikonal equation and a linear velocity gradient.** Show that for an isotropic medium the eikonal equation reduces to $\|\nabla T\|=1/\alpha$ and $H=\tfrac12\alpha^2\|\mathbf p\|^2$. For $\alpha(z)=\alpha_0+gz$ in 2D solve Hamilton's equations for a ray leaving the origin at take-off angle $\theta_0$ from the vertical: horizontal slowness $p_x=\sin\theta_0/\alpha_0$ conserved; show the ray is a circular arc of radius $R=1/(g p_x)$ centred at depth $z=-\alpha_0/g$; travel time $T=\tfrac1g\ln\!\big[\tan(\theta/2)/\tan(\theta_0/2)\big]$ along the ray. Numerical check by integrating Hamilton's equations (`python`, optional figure of the ray fan).
2. **From Hamilton's equations to the ray equation.** From $H=\tfrac12\alpha^2\|\mathbf p\|^2$ derive $\mathrm d\mathbf x/\mathrm d\sigma=\alpha^2\mathbf p$, $\mathrm d\mathbf p/\mathrm d\sigma=-\|\mathbf p\|^2\alpha\nabla\alpha$; show $\mathrm ds/\mathrm d\sigma=\alpha$ and hence $\frac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm ds}\big(\frac1\alpha\frac{\mathrm d\mathbf x}{\mathrm ds}\big)=\nabla\big(\frac1\alpha\big)$; deduce that rays are straight when $\alpha$ is constant and that they bend towards regions of low velocity (curvature $=-\hat{\mathbf n}\cdot\nabla\ln\alpha$... agent: derive the standard curvature formula $\kappa_{\rm ray}=\|\hat{\mathbf t}\times\nabla\ln\alpha\|$ direction toward decreasing $\alpha$).
3. **Transport equation in one dimension.** Rod with $\rho(x)$, $E(x)$: equation $\rho\ddot u=\partial_x(E\partial_x u)$. 5. **Where is the approximation? (short, qualitative + one calculation).** For the plane wave entering the heterogeneous region, estimate the size of the neglected $n=1$ term relative to $n=0$: $|a^1|/(\omega|a^0|)\sim 1/(\omega\tau)$ with $\tau=L/\alpha$, i.e. $\lambda/(2\pi L)$; give numbers for $L=1000$ km, $\alpha=10$ km/s, period 20 s.

2. **Free–free rod.** $\rho\ddot u=E u''$ on $[0,L]$ with $u'(0)=u'(L)=0$: eigenfunctions $\cos(k\pi x/L)$, $\omega_k=k\pi c/L$; normalise w.r.t. $\int\rho\,\cdot\,\mathrm dx$; show the $k=0$ (rigid translation) mode has $\langle 0|f\rangle=\int\overline S\,\partial_xw=0$ for the constant test function, so it is not excited by a stress glut; write the response to a point stress glut $\overline S=M_0\delta(x-x_s)H(t)$ using the Problem-Set-2 formula (check that it satisfies the initial conditions and, by direct substitution, the equation).
3. **Verify the time-domain mode sum.** Show by direct substitution that $u(t)=\sum_k\frac{\langle k|f\rangle(1-\cos\omega_kt)}{\omega_k^2}|k\rangle$ satisfies $P\ddot u+Hu=fH(t)$ with zero initial conditions (using completeness $\sum_k|k\rangle\langle k|P=1$ and $H|k\rangle=\omega_k^2P|k\rangle$).
4. **Spectral resolution and record length.** Fourier transform of a boxcar-windowed cosine of duration $T$: peak width $\approx1/T$ (define full width at half maximum); $_0S_2$ singlets are split by about $\pm 10\,\mu$Hz (agent: use $f_0\approx0.3094$ mHz and a total splitting width of about 30 μHz as illustrative); estimate the record length needed to resolve them (tens of hours) and compare with the Q-decay time scale ($Q\approx510$ for $_0S_2$: $\tau=Q/(\pi f)\approx 6$ days). `python` figure optional.
5. **Pointwise stability of an isotropic solid.** Show $A_{ijkl}e_{ij}^*e_{kl}=\kappa|\mathrm{tr}\,\mathbf e|^2+2\mu\,\mathbf e^{\rm dev}:\mathbf e^{{\rm dev}*}$ and hence that pointwise stability holds iff $\kappa>0$ and $\mu>0$; show this implies $\alpha>\beta>0$ but that the converse fails (give $\lambda=-0.9\mu$ as a counter-example); explain the physical difference between the two conditions (a homogeneous compression versus wave propagation).
